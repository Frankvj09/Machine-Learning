Linear Regression: Basic Concepts

Understanding the fundamentals of linear regression in machine learning

What is Linear Regression?

Linear regression is a statistical method used to model the relationship between a dependent variable (target) and one or more independent variables (predictors). It assumes a linear relationship between the variables, meaning that changes in the independent variables result in proportional changes in the dependent variable.

y = mx + b
y = dependent variable (what we want to predict)
x = independent variable (input)
m = slope (rate of change)
b = y-intercept (value when x = 0)

Variables: Independent & Dependent

Independent Variable (X): Also called predictor or feature. This is the input variable that we use to make predictions. Example: Study hours.

Dependent Variable (Y): Also called target or response. This is what we want to predict. Example: Final grade.

Example: In our student grade prediction model, study hours (X) influence final grade (Y). We use X to predict Y.

Training Data

Training data consists of known examples with both independent and dependent variables. The model learns from this historical data to find the best relationship between X and Y. In our example, we used 20 student records with their study hours and final grades to train the model.

Example training data:
Student 1: Study hours = 10 → Final grade = 3.8
Student 2: Study hours = 15 → Final grade = 4.2
Student 3: Study hours = 8 → Final grade = 3.0
The model learns from all these examples.

Regression Line (Best Fit Line)

The regression line is the line that best represents the relationship between X and Y in the training data. It's calculated to minimize the distance between the line and all data points. This line allows us to predict Y for any new X value.

Goal: Find the line that minimizes the sum of squared errors (SSE)
SSE = Σ(y_actual - y_predicted)²

Slope (m) and Intercept (b)

Slope (m): Measures how much Y changes for each unit change in X. A positive slope means Y increases as X increases. In our model, slope = 0.18, meaning each additional hour of study increases grade by 0.18 points.

Intercept (b): The value of Y when X = 0. In our model, intercept = 1.5, meaning a student who studies 0 hours is predicted to get a grade of 1.5.

Our model: Grade = 0.18 × (Study Hours) + 1.5
Example: 10 hours → Grade = 0.18 × 10 + 1.5 = 3.3

Real-World Application Example

Business Problem: A company wants to predict employee salary based on years of experience to ensure fair compensation.

Data: Collected salary data from 100 employees with their years of experience.

Model: Salary = m × (Years of Experience) + b

Result: After training, the model found that each additional year of experience increases salary by $5,000 on average (slope), with a base salary of $40,000 (intercept).

Prediction: A candidate with 5 years of experience would be predicted to earn: $40,000 + (5 × $5,000) = $65,000

Key Takeaways

  • ✓ Linear regression finds the best straight-line relationship between variables
  • ✓ Independent variables (X) are inputs, dependent variables (Y) are outputs
  • ✓ Training data is needed to learn the relationship
  • ✓ The regression line minimizes prediction errors
  • ✓ Slope tells us the rate of change, intercept tells us the starting point
  • ✓ Simple linear regression uses one variable, multiple uses several variables
